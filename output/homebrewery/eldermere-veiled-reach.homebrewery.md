# The Veiled Reach

The **Veiled Reach** is a small, deliberately magic-poor highland theocracy devoted to **Vaethrin**, ruled by an aging council called the **Curia of Watchers**. Its defining law is simple: Refined Wellspring is to be hoarded, not used. The doctrine says profligate magic caused the Graywake; restraint is worship.

The Reach is an isolated plateau in the Cinderspine's southern reach, touching both Cael Ambrose and the Ferrant Concord. Both powers want influence over it. Neither has managed to take it. The only reliable pass is controlled by the Reach itself, and the plateau's short growing season means it depends on trade-in-kind from below.

## Campaign Hooks

### The Curia's Split
The council is divided between reformists who want to spend the hoard on real defense and orthodox Watchers who insist the doctrine must hold whatever the cost. A party might be hired to protect a reformist petition, steal evidence of corruption, or escort a neutral envoy through a city built to refuse outsiders.

### The Observatory
Watchers who fast for three days enter the observatory and see lives that never happened: dead kin alive, gods still answering, streets that should not exist. The Curia calls these futures. They are not.

### The Hoard
Cairnholt stores the largest known stockpile of Refined Wellspring outside the great powers. While untouched, it is inert. Spent all at once, it would be the loudest magical event since the Graywake.

### Article Nine Spoilage
The Reach obsessively records inert supplies, failed lamps, and dated spoilage events. Filed as an archival nuisance, these records may be the best dataset in Eldermere on Vhorrek's movements.

# Settlements

## Veiled Rest
*Capital, population about 6,000*

Veiled Rest is built inside a still-partially-active Second Dominion observatory. It behaves like a monastery and a siege camp at once: low houses, narrow windows, whitewashed stone, hooded lanterns, ration bells, and ledgers for nearly everything.

**Settlement identity.** Veiled Rest is the Reach's argument made physical: knowledge preserved, appetite denied, beauty treated as temptation unless it can be filed.

### Key Sites

**The Curia Galleries.** A tiered circular hall where reformists sit low near the petition rail and orthodox Watchers sit high near the brass ribs. A petition spoken from below has to climb.

**The Three-Day Fast.** Candidates drink water, eat salt at sunset, and enter the inner theatre on the fourth dawn. Too many tried to walk toward what they saw, so now they are strapped into the observation chair.

**The Ledger Vaults.** Births, deaths, visitors, lamp oil, petitions, spoilage, and absences in older hands are all recorded. A false number is sin. So is omitting a number because it embarrasses someone important.

**The Quiet Courts.** Trials are seated, recorded, and repeated by clerks. The procedure began as mercy. It can now make urgency look like guilt.

\column

## Cairnholt
*Vault town, population about 2,200*

Cairnholt is a quarry town, mule town, storehouse town, and secret vault. Most residents think they maintain a practical logistics hub. Perhaps nine people know what the deepest chambers really hold.

**Settlement identity.** Cairnholt is the Reach's clenched fist. It exists to store what doctrine refuses to spend.

### Key Sites

**The Nine-Lock Vault.** Publicly a tithe cellar; actually the hidden Wellspring vault. Nine keepers, nine keys, nine spoken inventory lines.

**The Mule Courts.** Every sack entering the Reach is weighed, questioned, and converted into ledger value. Outside leverage becomes visible here.

**The Empty Count.** A yearly ritual where storehouses are opened and declared sufficient. Citizens leave reassured by an honest inventory of the wrong things.

## Thanewell
*Farming village, population about 1,800*

Thanewell is the dependent village that starved rather than see the hoard spent. Around two hundred died in the famine forty years ago. Its terraces, seed records, and petitions are now the reformists' strongest argument.

**Current pressure.** Thanewell makes the cosmic secret playable because every divine-scale decision becomes bread, ink, roads, medicine, and grief.

# More Places

## The Sisters' Gate
*Pass fortress, population about 400*

The Sisters' Gate controls the only reliable road into the Reach. It is less a fort than a hinge: two stone towers, a chain bridge, search yards, slate stables, and a cold yard where every cart is unloaded twice.

**Pass Law.** No one enters without a declared purpose, an internal sponsor, a witnessed inventory, and compliance with lamp and Wellspring restrictions. This once prevented cruelty and chaos. Now it can refuse a starving family more easily than a broker with perfect papers.

**The Bare Table Inn.** The only legal inn at the Gate. Guests eat the same stew as guards and sleep under numbered blankets. Every denied traveler, anxious merchant, and tired driver passes through its hall.

**The Bribe Road.** A goat path below the official pass, used by smugglers, reformist couriers, desperate petitioners, and anyone willing to make danger do the work of mercy.

## Coldmere Rise
*Hermitage, population about 150*

Coldmere Rise cares for Watchers who fasted and returned wrong. It is quiet, warm, and unusually tender for the Reach. Several residents can describe lives they never lived.

**The Gentle Rule.** No resident is contradicted at first telling. No vision-life is mocked. No sharp tool is left unattended. No Watcher may remove a resident for questioning without the Matron's consent.

**The False Families.** Residents remember spouses, children, neighbors, and streets that do not exist in the present world. Staff ask after them gently and record names only with permission.

# Secrets For The DM

The Reach's public doctrine is wrong in theology and right in survival. Loud magic draws attention. The Curia has the correct instinct and the wrong explanation.

## Vaethrin's Bunker
Vaethrin did not find the Veiled Reach. He founded it through an instrument in the first Curia. The hoarding doctrine, controlled pass, rationed lamps, ledgers, austerity, and magic-poor routines are load-bearing architecture around a wounded god hiding from inspection.

Joss Thane is therefore more dangerous than he knows. A good reformer trying to prevent starvation is also an existential threat to a hidden god.

## What The Observatory Shows
The observatory does not show futures. It renders the world as it would have continued if the Graywake had never happened. Fasters see ordinary grief from an impossible world: living relatives, casual divine names, and a history that was erased everywhere else.

The machine can likely be re-aimed. It is also nearly spent. Years remain, not decades.

## The Hoard As Weapon
The Cairnholt hoard is inert while stored. If spent all at once, it becomes a beacon. It could draw Vhorrek to a fixed and chosen location.

That makes the hoard a bomb, a lure, and a god-killing instrument. The Reach was built to protect Vaethrin and accidentally accumulated the perfect tool for his destruction.
